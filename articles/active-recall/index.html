Active Recall: A Simple Guide for Students

Active recall means trying to bring information back from memory before looking at the answer. It feels harder than rereading, which is exactly why it is useful.

What to do

Why rereading feels productive

Familiar text is easy to process, so it creates a feeling of knowing. That feeling is not the same as being able to answer a question without help.

A basic routine

Read a small section, close the book, write or say everything you remember, then reopen and compare. Mark what you missed.

Turn notes into questions

A heading like “Causes of osmosis” becomes “What are three causes of osmosis?” Question cards are more useful than definition cards alone.

Use flashcards carefully

Keep cards short and answer one question each. If a card is always easy, set it aside; if it is always difficult, rewrite it more simply.

Combine with explanations

Recall gets the answer out. Explaining why it is correct reveals gaps in understanding.

These are general study suggestions, not official exam regulations. Follow your institution’s guidance for exams and assessments.
