Study Planning

Focus Streak & Habits

Small repeated actions beat occasional cramming. Log a completed habit for any date, then use the streak counter, longest run and 35-day heatmap to see whether your routine is actually happening. Every entry is stored locally in your browser.

How the calculation works

A day counts once it has at least one logged habit. The current streak counts consecutive days ending today, or ending yesterday if today is not yet complete. The longest streak is the greatest run of consecutive logged days in your history. The heatmap shows the number of habits completed on each of the last 35 days, with darker cells for busier days.

Worked example

Example: Logging habits on 23, 24 and 25 November gives a current streak of 3 days and a longest streak of at least 3. If the next entry is on 27 November, the current streak resets to 1 but the longest streak stays 3.

How to use this page

  1. Type the habit you completed and choose the date it happened.
  2. Select Log completion. You can log the same habit on many different days.
  3. Review the current streak, longest streak, total active days and 35-day heatmap.
  4. Remove any mistaken entry from the history, export a CSV or clear everything.
Everything on this page runs in your browser. Your entries are not uploaded or stored on a server.

Frequently asked questions

Does a missed day break the streak?

Yes. The current streak only continues while each previous calendar day has at least one logged habit. The longest streak is never lost.

Can I log several habits on one day?

Yes. Multiple entries on the same date make that heatmap cell darker but still count as one day for streaks.

Can I backfill earlier dates?

Yes. Choose any past date before logging the completion.

Where is this history stored?

In this browser’s IndexedDB together with your other local task collections. It is never uploaded.
